About Me

I'm a communication graduate and former Division I women's lacrosse student-athlete who loves learning, collaborating, and taking on new challenges. A lifetime of team sports taught me communication, collaboration, and time management.

I earned my B.S. in Communication from East Carolina University. Now I'm pursuing my Master of Arts in Educational Innovation, Technology, and Entrepreneurship (MEITE) at UNC–Chapel Hill, exploring how new ideas can create better experiences for learners and organizations.

I'm also interning with Innovate Carolina at Launch Chapel Hill, where I develop founder case studies highlighting startup growth and the impact of the Launch Powered by KPMG accelerator. I interview entrepreneurs, build relationships with the business community, and research accelerator best practices to support entrepreneurship education programming.

Report · September 2026

Market Research Report Evaluation

Introduction

This evaluation analyzes the Coursera Global Skills Report 2025, which draws on data from more than 170 million learners in 109 countries. I wrote it in September 2026 for EDUC 590: Special Topics at UNC–Chapel Hill. It examines how generative AI, uneven AI readiness, and skills-based hiring are reshaping higher education and workforce development, explains why I agree with Coursera’s findings, and considers what they could mean over the next five to ten years. It was well received by my instructor.

Report · September 2026

Education Venture Report

Introduction

This report evaluates Coursera as an education venture, using its 2025 Form 10-K to examine its business model, financial position, and strategic outlook. I wrote it in September 2026 for EDUC 590: Special Topics at UNC–Chapel Hill. It finds that Coursera is growing, with $757.5 million in 2025 revenue and a shrinking net loss, but must prove its value against AI-powered learning tools, and it recommends how to strengthen its credentials. It was well received by my instructor.

Case study · 2025

SeaWorld and Blackfish Case Study

Introduction

This case study examines how SeaWorld responded to the reputational crisis that followed the 2013 documentary Blackfish. Using the PESO model (paid, earned, shared, and owned media), it evaluates SeaWorld’s key messages and other tactics, such as ending its orca breeding program, and judges them on whether they were timely, effective, and ethical. I wrote it in 2025 as a communication student at East Carolina University. It was well received.
